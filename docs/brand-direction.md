# Brand & Design Direction — Blue Willow Mental Health (2026-10-02)

Design strategy for the site rebuild. Goal: a brand that sells a mid-market ($225/$100) cash-pay ADHD practice to St. Matthews / Louisville — competent and calm, never cheap-looking, never luxury-priced.

## The market (grounded)
- St. Matthews: median household income **$82,412**, per-capita **$55,655** (Census QuickFacts 2020–2024); **58.7%** hold a bachelor's degree or higher; median age ~38; 71.8% in the labor force.
- Translation: educated, employed, health-literate buyers who research before they buy, are comfortable with telehealth, and can comfortably pay $225 cash for the right specialist. They comparison-shop — and they punish vague pricing.

## The clientele (two segments, one funnel)
1. **Parents of adolescents (roughly 35–50).** Kid is struggling in school; they suspect ADHD. They Google "ADHD testing Louisville" at 10pm. They want answers fast — not a 6-month waitlist, not a $2,500 neuropsych battery. Often arriving after the pediatrician route stalled.
2. **Adults (roughly 25–45), self-directed.** Suspect they've had ADHD all along. Many tried antidepressants that didn't work and want a real consult + medication change from someone who specializes. They've done their research and they compare providers.

## What these buyers need (the psychology)
- **Clarity:** "Do I / does my kid have ADHD?" — a definitive answer, not therapy-speak.
- **Speed:** book this week, no insurance authorization maze.
- **Credibility:** licensed specialist + *objective* testing. The computer-based screener is the differentiator — "objective data, not just a checklist."
- **Low friction:** telehealth from home, clear pricing upfront, pay before the visit.
- **Privacy:** telehealth + a non-eponymous brand = discreet. For adults, cash pay is a *feature* — no ADHD on the employer's insurance record. Say that out loud on the site.

## Positioning
**The focused ADHD specialist for Louisville — clear answers, honest prices, no games.**
Not the $3,000 battery. Not the 15-minute med check. The middle that respects both the buyer's intelligence and their wallet.

## Mid-market pricing strategy (how the design sells the price)
- **Publish the prices.** $225 / $100 right on the site. Mid-market buyers comparison-shop; hiding prices loses them. Transparency is the trust signal.
- **Anchor against the expensive alternative:** "A full neuropsych battery in Louisville runs $1,500–$3,500. Our focused ADHD assessment is $225." Value frame, not discount frame.
- **Frame cash pay as the feature:** "No insurance. No surprise bills. No prior authorizations. Just care."
- **Never look cheap** (cheap = "is she legit?") and **never look luxury** (luxury = "can I afford this?"). The brand target: *the wise specialist.*

## Design system
- **Palette** (willow + trust, warm not clinical):
  - Willow Deep `#1E4D4B` — primary, headlines, header
  - Sage `#A8BFA5` — secondary, dividers, calm backgrounds
  - Cream `#FAF7F0` — page background (warm, not stark white)
  - Amber `#D9A441` — accent, CTAs, highlights
  - Ink `#22302E` — body text
  - Avoid: cold hospital blues, aggressive reds, millennial-pink therapy clichés.
- **Typography:** Fraunces (serif, headlines — established, professional) + Inter (sans, body — clean, modern). Both Google Fonts, Astro-friendly.
- **Imagery:** real, calm, human. Soft natural light, willow/greenery motifs, everyday settings — teens studying, adults working from home. No stock-photo handshakes, no head-in-hands clichés. Show the *comfort* of telehealth-from-home.
- **Voice:** direct, warm, plain-spoken. "Answers, not appointments." Write for the 10pm Googler.
- **Logo:** client's Facebook page mark — to be placed once the file is in hand. Willow-blue mark should sit comfortably on Cream with Willow Deep text.

## Homepage blueprint (conversion flow)
1. **Hero:** "Is it ADHD? Get a clear answer." / Sub: focused ADHD assessments for teens & adults — telehealth across Kentucky, $225 flat. CTA: **Book your assessment** (SimplePractice embed) + secondary "How it works."
2. **Trust strip:** licensed provider · transparent cash pricing · telehealth · objective computer-based screening.
3. **How it works (3 steps):** Book online → complete the computer-based screener → 60–90 min review. Leave with clarity and a plan.
4. **Who it's for (two cards):** Parents — "Is it ADHD or something else?" / Adults — "Tried antidepressants, still struggling? Get a specialist's second opinion."
5. **Pricing (transparent cards):** Intake $225 · Follow-up $100. Anchor line vs. $1,500+ batteries. "No insurance. No surprise bills."
6. **FAQ:** How fast can I be seen? Is telehealth effective for ADHD assessment? What if it isn't ADHD? Can I use HSA/FSA? Do you prescribe? (Scope answers to her license.)
7. **About the practice:** "Blue Willow is led by a licensed psychiatric provider specializing in ADHD." — no personal name, per her rule.
8. **Final CTA + booking embed.** Footer: HIPAA/privacy note, Psychology Today badge, service area (St. Matthews · Louisville · telehealth Kentucky).

## What to avoid
- Eponymous branding (her rule: name stays off).
- Symptom-checklist quizzes on the site (liability + cheapens the clinical screener).
- Health-detail intake on the contact form (HIPAA — contact info only; clinical intake lives in SimplePractice).
- Generic "mental wellness" stock aesthetics — she's a *specialist*, and the design must say so.
